Papers

Peter Cotton's papers and notes on epidemics.

Published

  • Cotton, P. (2020). A Fundamental Theorem for Epidemiology. Article, 27 May 2020. Doubling times under a week against peak infection near 20%: de Finetti's theorem reduces the question to mixtures of independent neighbourhoods, and the susceptible share at the peak to a harmonic mean.
  • Cotton, P. (2020). Repeat Contacts and the Spread of Disease: An Agent Model with Compartmental Solution. Preprint. arXiv:2005.10311. SIR with transmission attenuated by the probability that a contact is new, derived from agents moving in the plane. How fast acquaintances go stale changes final sizes by an order of magnitude.
  • Cotton, P. (2020). Addressing the Herd Immunity Paradox Using Symmetry, Convexity Adjustments and Bond Prices. Preprint. arXiv:2006.07341. The growth convexity from the moment generating function of the transmission rate, or Vasicek's bond formula for an Ornstein–Uhlenbeck rate, and the harmonic-mean convexity at the peak. Summarized on mixtures and convexity.
  • Cotton, P. (2020). How Population Shape Tilts Your Odds of Getting COVID-19. Article. When contagiousness varies with population density, Jensen's inequality lowers the herd-immunity threshold through a harmonic mean, with a closed form when contagiousness is a power of density.
  • Cotton, P. (2020). Got Milk? On Homogenization and Survival Probability. Article. Survival under a hazard rate that switches between regimes, with infection risk for commuters as the opening example; the mathematics is developed at homogenization.microprediction.org.

Working papers and notes

  • Cotton, P. (2026). Where Epidemics Turn: The Immunity Factor from Contact Surveys, County Waves and Serology. Notes in preparation. Summary · code and log. The rule $1 - R^{-1/\lambda}$; closed forms for time-varying activity; $\lambda$ from contact surveys with nothing fitted; tests on US counties, Spain, Sweden, England, Geneva and Manaus.
